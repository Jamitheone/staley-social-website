Signs your marketing agency isn't tracking conversions correctly

November 16, 2026

You're paying a marketing agency to help you grow your business, but are you really getting the results you need? With conversion tracking, it's possible that your agency is misinterpreting or underreporting the actual number of leads generated from your marketing efforts. This can lead to wasted time and resources on ineffective campaigns, or even worse, a complete lack of understanding of what's working and what's not.

If your marketing agency isn't tracking conversions correctly, you might notice that your website traffic is low, but your sales team is still reporting a steady stream of new leads. On the other hand, you might see a spike in website traffic, but your sales team is struggling to close deals. These discrepancies can be a red flag that something is amiss in your conversion tracking.

Another sign that your marketing agency might not be tracking conversions correctly is if they're using vague or inaccurate language to describe the effectiveness of their campaigns. Are they using terms like "impressions" or "click-through rates" without explaining how those numbers translate into actual sales or leads? This can be a sign that they're not taking the time to really understand your business goals and what metrics truly matter.

A clear sign that your marketing agency is tracking conversions incorrectly is if they're not regularly providing you with detailed, actionable data about your marketing performance. Do they send you regular reports or dashboards that break down the numbers and show you exactly what's working and what's not? Or do you have to constantly ask for this information, only to be met with vague promises that "the numbers are looking good"?

If your marketing agency is relying too heavily on assumptions or gut feelings when it comes to tracking conversions, you might want to take a closer look at their approach. Are they using generic templates or assumptions to estimate the effectiveness of their campaigns, rather than actually testing and measuring the results? This can be a recipe for disaster, as it leaves room for bias and inaccuracy to creep in.

Finally, if your marketing agency is consistently unable to provide you with clear, data-driven recommendations for improving your marketing performance, it may be time to start looking elsewhere. Do they seem more interested in selling you a new service or product than in actually helping you improve your business? Or do they seem hesitant or unclear when you ask them to provide specific guidance on how to improve your marketing metrics? This can be a sign that they're not invested in your success, and that you deserve better.
